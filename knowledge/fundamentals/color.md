# Color

## Ramps, not colors
Define each material or hue as a ramp of 4-7 steps from dark to light. Shade by moving along the ramp, never by adding black or white.
- **Hue shift**: shadows rotate toward cool (blue, violet) or toward the ambient color; highlights rotate toward warm (yellow) or the light color. Typical: 10-25° of hue between neighboring steps at the ends of the ramp.
- **Saturation arc**: saturation peaks in the mid and half-light steps; the darkest and lightest steps are less saturated. Dark steps that stay saturated look muddy; light steps that stay saturated look neon.
- **Warm light, cool shadow** (or the inverse for cold light), stated in the art bible and used everywhere.
- Gold's shadows are warm brown-red, not gray. Green's shadows lean blue-teal. Red's shadows lean violet-crimson. Skin and wood shadows lean red.

## Budget
- 60/30/10: one dominant ramp, one supporting ramp, accents at about 10%.
- Accent colors carry meaning: primary action (CTA), rarity, danger, selection. Do not spend them on decoration.
- The primary CTA should be the highest-contrast, most saturated element on its screen.
- Emissive colors (energy, magic, fire, holograms) are the only place for near-white cores and high saturation together.

## Rarity convention
Common gray or white, uncommon green, rare blue, epic purple, legendary orange or gold, mythic red or pink. Adapt the exact hues to the art bible; keep the order so players can read it.

## Harmony and context
- Analogous ramps with one complementary accent are the safest harmony.
- Backgrounds and large surfaces: lower saturation and contrast than interactive elements.
- Check assets against the game's actual backdrop colors (the review sheet shows dark and light); UI must read on both unless the art bible says otherwise.
- Semitransparent colors pick up what is behind them; prefer opaque colors for UI chrome and reserve transparency for glass, glow and holograms.
