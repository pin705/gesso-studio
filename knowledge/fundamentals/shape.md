# Shape, silhouette and composition

## Silhouette first
Fill the asset with a single dark color in your head: it must still read. A unique, readable silhouette is the difference between an icon a player recognizes mid-fight and a blob.

## Shape language
- **Circles and curves**: friendly, safe, soft, magical, casual.
- **Squares and rectangles**: stable, strong, trustworthy, defensive (shields, panels, stone, tech).
- **Triangles and points**: danger, speed, aggression, magic, sci-fi.
Pick a dominant shape family for the project in the art bible and use the others as accents.

## Big, medium, small
One big shape, two or three medium shapes, then small details. Vary sizes and spacing (roughly 60/30/10). Equal sizes and equal spacing ("twins") look mechanical. Avoid tangents: shapes that barely touch or edges that line up by accident create visual tension; overlap clearly or separate clearly.

## Detail hierarchy
About 70% rest area, 20% medium detail, 10% high detail at the focal point. The focal point also gets the strongest value contrast and sharpest edges. Detail that is invisible at display size is noise: check the 64px preview.

## Composition
- Long items (swords, staffs, wands, arrows) sit diagonally, bottom-left to top-right, which reads dynamic and uses the square canvas well.
- Rounded items (potions, shields, orbs) sit upright, maybe tilted 5-10° for life.
- Slight 3/4 view beats flat front view for objects; UI chrome is front-on.
- Leave negative space around the silhouette; icons fill about 80-90% of the box.

## Ornament
Ornament grows from the structure: it follows edges, caps corners, reinforces joints. Pick two or three motifs from the art bible and repeat them consistently. Ornament stays in the corners of 9-slice assets so edges can stretch.

## Consistency across a kit
Same corner radius family (for example 6, 12, 24), same bevel depth, same outline weights, same ornament motifs and the same light direction for every asset in the set.
